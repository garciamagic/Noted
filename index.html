Instructions

Noted looks like an ordinary note with a scribble in it. The name your spectator thinks of turns up in a list of past predictions hidden below it.

Part 1: Setting Up

  1. Put it on your home screen. Upload the Noted folder to your host (the same way as OneLock), open the link in Safari, then tap Share → Add to Home Screen. It shows up as “Noted”.
  2. Allow the mic and motion. Tap ••• at the top right, then tap Allow for Microphone and Motion under Before You Perform. Do this again any time you've fully closed Noted.
  3. Check your settings. The Note is what they see (Scribble by default). The Prediction is the hidden list. Listening has the phrases you'll say.
  4. Practice. Run the routine out loud. The orange dot at the top of the screen should come on after your double-tap and go off after your lock word.

Part 2: The Scribble Routine

  1. Open the note. On the Notes list, tap the note with the scribble (it looks like you made it earlier in the week). Double-tap anywhere on it to start listening; the orange dot appears at the top. Have them look at the scribble.
  2. Ask. “Who's the first person that comes to mind when you look at this? Does it remind you of anyone?” If they say “my mom” or “my sister”, ask “What's her name?”
  3. They say a name. Smile: “Interesting.” The mic turns off.
  4. Show the list. Hand them the phone and have them scroll down to the list of Maybe's, with YES or NO next to each. “Sometimes when I look at this scribble, names flash in my head and I jot them down. Look at the one I jotted down this morning.” It's their name.
  5. Mark it. Tap the end of that line and type “ - YES”, like the others.
  6. Point to the next name. The last line, right under the prediction, is Owen, one that “hasn't happened yet”.

Part 3: The Second Person (Dual Reality)

  1. Scroll back up to the scribble. Turning the phone only works after you've been down to the list and back.
  2. Stop a random person. “Excuse me… can you help us out real quick? Who does this remind you of?” As you ask, turn the phone toward them. They see the scribble with three names under it: Derek, Owen and Patrick.
  3. They say Owen. Turn the phone back to your spectator. The names are gone, so your spectator only ever sees the plain scribble. It switches while the screen is edge-on during the turn, and only once.

Why it works: your prediction was on screen before the second person was even stopped, so a hidden microphone can't explain it. Owen is always the last line, right under the prediction. Each saved prediction goes above the next one, so Owen stays last.

Tip: When you go back to the Notes list, the prediction stays in the list and everything resets for the next performance. Dual reality needs Motion allowed; hand wobble and turning the phone face down never set it off.

Part 4: Other Options

  1. Use a poem, your own text, or a photo. Pick it under The Note. With the YOU poem, have them read the first two stanzas to themselves, then ask the same question.
  2. Ask their name too. Turn it on under Listening. Ask “What's your name?” at any time and say “Right on.” The prediction then reads like “Alina will think of Kate”. The mic turns off after each name, so double-tap before asking for the other.
  3. Sound unsure. Under The Prediction, adds a near miss: “Maybe Cat or Kate”.
  4. Name Mode. For the YOU poem or your own text: the name goes right into the last line instead of the list. The list becomes the backup if the name is missed.
